# MIT Solve · 2027 Global Learning Challenge

Project reference for Bridge. The challenge guidance and prize details below come from the supplied MIT Solve materials. [Official challenge page](https://solve.mit.edu/index.php/challenges/2027-global-learning-challenge)

## Judge demo and application evidence

The two-minute statistics demo follows one learner from an unfamiliar academic phrase to an instructor’s teaching decision. Choose Español for the gloss, answer the two concept checks, and choose “Not sure” for “respectively.” Try the new question, go back to the original sentence, and open the instructor view. All eight attempts in that view are fictional. [Judge demo link](https://zelinzhu-richard.github.io/bridge/#solve-demo)

| Criterion | Evidence to present |
| --- | --- |
| Alignment | The quick check separates academic language from concepts. The optional Spanish gloss and suggested instructor lesson address the same reading barrier. |
| Potential for impact | The learner interprets the original sentence after a fresh recheck. The pilot will measure independent passage explanations and unseen transfer after one week. |
| Feasibility | The saved demo works without an account or AI connection. The first pilot uses one instructor, one adult statistics section, and approved readings. |
| Innovative approach | The demo identifies what the learner already knows, teaches only the missing prerequisite, and returns them to the assigned text. |
| Human-centered design | Test with the instructor and three to five adult learners before using the activity in a section. Record what they changed and which glosses a qualified speaker reviewed. |
| Scalability | Use the same approved activity across devices. Record instructor preparation time, generation usage, and delivery cost before estimating wider rollout. |
| Partnership potential | Seek an instructor to adapt course material, a bilingual reviewer, and an evaluator for independent scoring. Record confirmed contributions as they occur. |
| Technical feasibility | Follow the link through quick check, explanation, recheck, original reading, and Teacher view. Saved content and fictional class counts show the workflow. |

The classroom comparison will use matched passages and ordinary rereading as the comparison condition. Capture what the instructor actually reteaches, how many participants complete each activity, and how many return for the delayed check.

## Judging criteria

- **Alignment with the Challenge.** The solution addresses the challenge that has been set forth using technology.
- **Potential for Impact.** The planned solution implementation has the potential to impact the intended population.
- **Feasibility.** The team has a realistic, practical plan for implementing the solution, and it is feasible in the given context.
- **Innovative Approach.** The solution includes a new technology, a new application of technology, a new business model, or a new process for solving the Challenge.
- **Human-Centered Design.** The solution is designed with and for underserved communities, and the solution team demonstrates that they have the experience, relationships, data, and knowledge to develop and deliver a successful solution.
- **Scalability.** The solution has a plan for financial viability and the potential to be scaled to affect the lives of more people.
- **Partnership Potential.** The applicant clearly explains how the solution would benefit from the broad range of resources that the Solve community is positioned to provide.
- **Technical Feasibility.** The applicant has provided convincing evidence that the data/analytics underpinning the solution have been built and function as they claim they do, even if only proof-of-concept.

## What this means for Bridge

- **Alignment and innovation.** Show how Bridge distinguishes academic language from STEM concepts, checks what a student already knows, and teaches the missing prerequisite before returning the student to the original course reading. The student still answers the questions and interprets the passage.
- **Potential for impact and human-centered design.** Focus on multilingual and first-generation students in introductory STEM at community colleges. In a pilot, work with students and instructors on actual assigned readings. Check whether students can explain the passage independently and apply a missed concept to a fresh question after a week.
- **Feasibility and technical evidence.** Present Bridge as a working prototype. Use the demo to show the learner flow, the Chinese and Spanish glosses, the read-aloud option, and the teacher view. Prototype checks and labeled example class data show how the tool works. Student outcomes require separate evidence.
- **Teacher judgment and access.** Explain how the teacher view helps instructors decide what to reteach. The current view summarizes anonymous sessions on one device. Describe the four saved lessons that run without live AI and the connection needed to analyze new passages. Address privacy, AI content review, and data handling in the pilot plan.
- **Scalability and partnerships.** Describe how the initial classroom pilot could inform a practical rollout, what it would cost to support live AI, and where Solve connections could help with educator review, pilot recruitment, and evaluation.

## Challenge description and priorities

Quality education remains central to improving individual and societal outcomes. Yet with fewer than four years left to reach SDG 4, the challenge of ensuring quality education for all [**remains steep**](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/The-Sustainable-Development-Goals-Report-2026.pdf): over 270 million children are out of school, one in five teachers don't meet minimum national qualification standards, and aid to education is on track [**to fall 30%**](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000398525) between 2023 and 2027.

EdTech is also at a pivotal moment. AI now offers learners an always-available tutor and gives teachers powerful new support. However, it can also let students bypass the productive struggle through which real learning happens, weaken peer and teacher relationships, and widen the gap between those with access to frontier tools and those without\*. [**Technology backlashes in classrooms**](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/29/technology/parents-school-tech-backlash.html), so far concentrated in higher-income countries, and [**decreasing investment**](https://restofworld.org/2026/edtech-funding-collapse-k12-startups-ai-workforce/) mean builders of education solutions must [**design for impact**](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/impact-by-design-the-emerging-era) and demonstrate [**clear educational value**](https://edtechpartnerships.substack.com/p/the-great-edtech-pivot-is-coming). That means showing gains in what learners can actually do, not just engagement or completion. Privacy, fairness, safety, human oversight, and accountable data governance must be built in, especially for tools serving children.

MIT Solve seeks exceptional solutions that wield technology to address and close global education gaps, with a particular interest in 2027 for solutions that:

- **Strengthen teachers’ instructional practice through evidence-grounded tools** that augment teachers’ judgment and relationships with learners rather than automate them, with a clear case for technology in and around classrooms
- **Work in emergency or low-resource settings**, including low-bandwidth, offline, or low-cost designs, and tools to bolster school- or district-level systems and capacity
- **Include assistive technology or multilingual capabilities** that better support learners with disabilities or learners using non-dominant languages
- **Improve assessment of what learners truly know and can do**, including formative assessment, diagnostics, or early screening that flag when a learner is off-track so a teacher can intervene

Across all areas, we will prioritize solutions that:

- Use AI and other technologies to deepen learning rather than shortcut it
- Foster peer, teacher, and community connection rather than isolation
- Are designed with, or led by, the communities they serve
- Integrate with current curricula and future interoperability
- Strengthen public education systems

Every person deserves an excellent education and the opportunities that it unlocks. Let’s make that a reality, together.

*\*See MIT’s [**AI and Education Report (August 2026)**](https://aiandeducation.mit.edu/report/) for more insights and recommendations on AI use in teaching and learning.*

## Eligibility and stages

Solve seeks innovative, human-centered, tech-based solutions to our Global Challenges. Through open innovation, Solve is looking for a diverse portfolio of solutions across geography and stage of development. We encourage people of all backgrounds to submit applications. MIT Solve adheres to the principles of nondiscrimination with respect to its programmatic activities and does not make any decisions on the basis of race, color, religion, creed, gender, gender expression, age, national origin, disability, marital status, or any other characteristic protected by law.

You can submit applications at the stages listed below.

- **Concept.** An idea being explored and researched for its feasibility to build a product, service, or business model, including prototypes under development. Until the solution has a functioning prototype, we would still consider it a Concept. Concept-stage ideas are only reviewed under the Indigenous Communities Fellowship.
- **Prototype.** An initial working version of a solution that may be in the process of getting initial feedback or testing with users (i.e. running a pilot). If for-profit, a solution that has raised little or no investment capital. Until the solution transitions from testing to consistent availability, we would still consider it a Prototype. (Often 0 users/direct beneficiaries)
- **Pilot.** The solution has been launched in at least one community, but is still iterating on design or business model. If for-profit, is generally working to gain traction and may have completed a fundraising round with investment capital. (Often 10+ users/direct beneficiaries)
- **Growth.** An established solution available in one or more communities with a consistent design and approach, ready for further growth in multiple communities or countries. If for-profit, has generally completed at least one formal investment round (Seed stage or later). If nonprofit, has an established set of donors and/or revenue streams.
- **Scale.** A standard solution operating in many communities or multiple countries, prepared to scale significantly by improving efficiency. If for-profit, has likely raised at least a Series A investment round.

We define technology broadly as the application of science and evidence-based knowledge to the practical aims of human life. We welcome solutions that are using apps, SMS technology, software, AI, robots, blockchain, and virtual reality! We also welcome solutions that leverage traditional, ancestral, and natural technologies and knowledge systems. Tech-based innovations across our Solver portfolio include plant-based solutions that reduce the effects of climate change, machine-learning platforms that improve the delivery of justice and governance, biodegradable sanitary pads, and low-cost solar-powered vehicles, to name a few. Learn about all [our Solver teams'](https://solve.mit.edu/innovators/meet-the-solvers) diverse technologies.

If you or your organization has been selected as a Solver for a Global or Custom Challenge in the past, including as an Indigenous Communities Fellow, you may still apply, but your solution must be new. It cannot be the same solution that was previously selected by Solve. Reach out to [help@solve.mit.edu](mailto:help@solve.mit.edu) if you have any questions about your eligibility.

## Application and selection

Review [How to Apply to a Challenge](https://solve.mit.edu/how-to-apply) for tips and tutorials to get started.

One team lead, solution, or organization can apply to multiple Global Challenges if there is alignment. However, if you advance beyond the initial judging rounds in multiple Global Challenges, you will be asked to select one challenge in which to advance.

A team lead cannot submit multiple solutions to the same Global Challenge. However, we encourage you to look at other open custom challenges on our site. You can apply to as many of these as you fit the application criteria for! Note that the resources offered to winners of Custom Challenges vary from the Solver program associated with the Global Challenges.

First, Solve's staff and community will conduct an initial screening. Secondly, expert reviewers will evaluate and score the solutions according to Solve’s judging criteria, and the solutions with the highest scores will pass to the semifinalist round. Challenge Leadership Group judges (experts and leaders from across industries and sectors) will evaluate and score the solutions. The highest-scoring solutions will become finalists. They will be notified in February and invited to a virtual live pitch and Q&A session, after which the judges will deliberate and select the Solver class.

### Evaluation criteria as listed in the supplied FAQ

- **Alignment.** The solution uses technology to address one of Solve's Global Challenges.
- **Potential for Impact.** The planned solution implementation has the potential to impact the intended population.
- **Feasibility.** The team has a realistic, practical plan for implementing the solution, and it is feasible in the given context.
- **Innovative Approach.** The solution includes a new technology, a new application of technology, a new business model, or a new process for solving the Challenge.
- **Human-Centered Design.** The solution is designed with and for underserved communities, and the solution team demonstrates that they have the experience, relationships, data, and knowledge to develop and deliver a successful solution.
- **Scalability\*.** The solution has a plan for financial viability and the potential to be scaled to affect the lives of more people.
- **Partnership Potential\*.** The applicant clearly explains how the solution would benefit from the broad range of resources that the Solve community is positioned to provide.
- **Technical Feasibility\*.** If the underlying technology is novel, the applicant has provided convincing evidence that it has been built and functions as they claim it does.

\*This criterion will only be considered during finalist selection and beyond.

Once you decide you would like to apply to Solve’s 2027 Global Challenges, begin your application as soon as you can. In post-application surveys, most applicants reported that they spent several hours completing the application. Be sure to budget enough time to submit your application before the deadline.

To help you prepare, download a [PDF version of the application](https://5593819.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/5593819/2027%20Global%20Challenges_%20Application%20Questions.pdf). The PDF version is only for reference; you must [submit your application online](https://solve.mit.edu/challenges#open-challenges).

## Solver program

Solver teams receive an average of $40k each following selection and because once a Solver, always a Solver, they will continue to be eligible for funding opportunities throughout their program year and beyond.

**Funding**

- Access to a pool of +$1M in prize funding at selection and additional funding opportunities throughout the program and after.

**Nine-month support program**

- Tailored capacity-building workshops that may cover marketing, public relations, monitoring and evaluation, business model, and more
- Leadership coaching
- Access to in-kind and pro bono resources such as software licenses and legal services
- Monitoring and evaluation support
- A powerful network of impact-minded leaders across industries and sectors, including MIT
- Attendance at events throughout the year for opportunities to pitch and connect
- Exposure in the media and potential speaking opportunities at conferences
- Connections with current and past Solver teams for peer-to-peer support, inspiration, and learning

Selected Solver teams should expect to commit ~25 hours to the program over the course of nine months, inclusive of coaching sessions, workshops, virtual events, etc. Additionally, attendance to MIT Solve flagship events is four days for Solver Orientation in Cambridge, MA (April) and four days for Solve Challenge Finals in New York City (September).

Once a Solver, always a Solver! You will continue to have access to funding opportunities, in-kind and pro bono resources, exposure in the media, and opportunities to attend Solve events and connect with the community.

Review the [events calendar](https://solve.mit.edu/events) for application clinics, where you can learn more about MIT Solve, the Solver program, commonly asked questions, and Solve's selection criteria. You can also email [help@solve.mit.edu](mailto:help@solve.mit.edu). Please allow 3–5 business days for a response.
